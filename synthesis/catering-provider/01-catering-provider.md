# Fawaz - The Catering Operations Lead

> *"I can cook anything. What I can't do is guess which train is coming, when, and to which platform."*

**Face.** A man in his 40s in a chef's apron over a polo shirt, standing at the stainless-steel pass of a small kitchen, a tablet on a stand showing the day's train orders and a phone in one hand.

**Goal.** Receive each order, prepare it, and hand it to the passenger — at an outlet inside or close to the station, or by sending staff with special passes into the carriage — with the right train, station, platform and arrival time in front of him when he needs it, and no more of the railway than that. When a journey changes, he wants to be told so he can decide whether the order can still be fulfilled.

**Pain.** He cannot plan preparation or be ready at the right time and place, because he acts on incomplete, late or missing journey information: the railway's live data is not connected to him in a timely, structured way and changes are not pushed to him. The handover happens in a short window, so even small timing errors become a missed or wasted order, and a small but material share of orders today risk being late, missed or wasted. Ownership of payment, cancellation and confirmation was never clearly defined — and when the train is late or cancelled, his reputation suffers for a delay that was not his fault. He also carries the no-show risk: because payment is collected on delivery, a passenger can order and not turn up, and he loses the food.

---

*Illustrative name and face. The goal and pain are taken from the external catering provider discovery.*
