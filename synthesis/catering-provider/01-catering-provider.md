# RailBites - The Catering Business

> *"We can cook anything. What we can't do is guess which train is coming, when, and to which platform."*

**Face.** A compact commercial kitchen and counter a short walk from a station entrance — a stainless-steel pass with insulated delivery bags stacked ready, a wall screen listing the day's train orders, and a handful of staff in matching uniforms with platform passes on lanyards. Fulfilment runs either from the counter or, where passes allow, onto the train itself.

**Goal.** Take each order, prepare it, and get it to the passenger — at the counter inside or close to the station, or into the carriage where the team can go — with the right train, station, platform and arrival time in front of the kitchen through the SmartRail integration, exactly when it is needed and no more of the railway than that. When a journey changes, be told so it can decide whether the order can still be fulfilled.

**Pain.** It cannot plan preparation or be ready at the right time and place, because it acts on incomplete, late or missing journey information: the railway's live data is not connected to it in a timely, structured way and changes are not pushed to it. The handover happens in a short window, so even small timing errors become a missed or wasted order, and a small but material share of orders today risk being late, missed or wasted. Ownership of payment, cancellation and confirmation was never clearly defined, and when a train is late or cancelled its reputation suffers for a delay that was not its fault. It also carries the no-show risk: because payment is collected on delivery, a passenger can order and not turn up, and the food is lost.

---

*Illustrative business name and face. The goal and pain are taken from the catering provider discovery.*
