# Mahesh - The Network Controller

> *"I'm supposed to know what every train is doing right now — and I'm the one who can't actually see it."*

**Face.** A controller in his 30s at a desk in the operations centre, headset on, three screens in front of him and a radio at his elbow, watching a picture he has to keep assembling by hand.

**Goal.** Run the network as it is today: monitor every train, log incidents, delay, cancel, skip a stop, terminate early, and allocate or change platforms and crew inside his 24–48 hour window — with a single, live view of the whole network and the tools to act on it. When a restriction has to outlast his window, he wants a tracked handoff to the Scheduler, with a deadline, escalation and a logged fallback.

**Pain.** There is no single live view of the network: he reads it from radio, phone and separate systems, so he is always acting on a partial, lagging account of his own railway. An incident he resolves can silently stop being anyone's problem. Anything beyond his window has to become a handoff to the Scheduler, and there is no shared, tracked path for it — so handoffs are missed, duplicated or completed without a record, and a live restriction can fail to become a future plan change, leaving trains running against a plan that no longer reflects reality: a closed station, a shut platform, a line under maintenance or a changed route. Decisions get relayed by hand, and only a minority of disruptions are ever visible to the centre as they happen.

---

*Illustrative name and face. The goal and pain are taken from the central control discovery.*
