# Kavindya - The Passenger Who Orders Food

> *"The food is the easy part. What I actually need to know is whether my train will still be there when it arrives."*

**Face.** A woman in her late 20s on a longer journey, tray table down, phone propped up, signed in to the app, scrolling the food options for the route ahead and watching the journey timer as much as the menu.

**Goal.** To act on her journey, not just read about it: discover the food options available on her route, order, and have the order delivered or collected at a suitable station during the journey — and then track it, the way she tracks her train.

**Pain.** She cannot discover or order food against her journey, or know whether an order will actually arrive; a service that could ease her journey is either unavailable or unreliable. Worse, the order only works on top of trustworthy live information: if the train is delayed or changed and she is not told, she and the provider fall out of step. Tracking is just as broken — she needs the order's progress to follow **placed, accepted, rejected by the provider, preparing, on its way, handed over, not picked up or cancelled**, in the same way she follows her train.

---

*Illustrative name and face. The goal and pain are taken from the passenger discovery.*
