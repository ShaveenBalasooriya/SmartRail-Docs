# Dinesh - The Passenger With Cognitive Needs

> *"There was something on the board, it flashed past, and now everyone's moving and I still don't know what changed."*

**Face.** A man in his 30s in a plain t-shirt, standing still in the middle of a moving crowd, phone angled toward him, reading one line at a time and re-reading it before he looks up.

**Goal.** Understand what is happening and what to do next — *which train, when, from where, is it on time, where is it now, will I make my connection* — in plain, uncomplicated terms, without having to decode it under pressure.

**Pain.** Information that is complex, too fast, or **in only one place** is hard to take in: a message that flashes once and disappears is lost, and a busy, noisy disruption — exactly when wording is densest and time is shortest — overloads him. He can miss a change not because it was never shown but because it was not clear, not repeated, or not available long enough to act on. Disruptions, when information matters most, expose the gap hardest.

---

*Illustrative name and face. The goal and pain are taken from the passenger discovery.*
