# Nimali - The Daily Commuter

> *"I know this line. I just need to know, right now, whether my train is on time — and if it isn't, what I should do about it."*

**Face.** A 30-something office worker in a light jacket and lanyard, one earbud in, phone in hand, standing near the platform edge at 07:45. She barely glances at the notice board; she already knows the timetable by heart.

**Goal.** Get to work — and home again — on time, without wasted waiting. She wants to answer the basic questions reliably, in the moment she needs them: *which train, when, from where, is it on time, where is it now, will I make my connection.*

**Pain.** As the most frequent traveller, she is the one most exposed to the recurring, everyday failures: daily uncertainty, sharpest at peak times and during disruptions, with wasted time on platforms, missed connections, standing on the wrong platform, and no confidence whether to wait or re-plan. When something changes she often learns of it late — sometimes only once she has already reached the station — and what she is told does not match between the display, the announcement and staff.

---

*Illustrative name and face. The goal and pain are taken from the passenger discovery.*
