# Ishara - The Onboard Guard

> *"I give the all-clear and the train goes — but the one moment I'm actually responsible for is the one nobody writes down."*

**Face.** A woman in railway uniform standing at the platform edge beside an open door, one hand raised toward the driver, eyes moving along the train as boarding finishes.

**Goal.** Confirm that boarding and alighting are complete and the train is safe, give the driver the all-clear, and have that "ready to depart" moment captured as the journey's honest, human-confirmed departure time — flowing to the controller and onward to passengers. During the stop she also wants to record what she sees — an unusually long dwell, blocked doors, overcrowding, assisted boarding, a passenger safety event, an emergency brake application — so that it is not lost.

**Pain.** Her departure and safety decisions exist only in the moment and in verbal exchange, with no digital record of what she confirmed or when. The "ready to depart" signal is verbal and visual only, so the live picture has to infer a departure rather than be told one, and arrival is never confirmed by a human at all — leaving her the only human source of an actual time. That means responsibility without evidence: extra verbal reporting to control, and exposure to blame when something goes wrong. As the point of contact for passenger safety and information at stops she has no digital tooling to record or escalate what she sees — and any tool she is given must not distract her from platform safety, and must survive weak connectivity, storing confirmations and events locally and syncing them when the network returns without losing their original timestamps, with the system showing estimated times until they sync.

---

*Illustrative name and face. The goal and pain are taken from the guard discovery and the driver-operator discovery.*
