# Nuwan - The Train Operator

> *"I can tell control what's happening — but only when the train's stopped, and by then it's a radio call someone else has to write down."*

**Face.** A man in his 40s in a railway uniform shirt, hands on the controls, eyes on the line ahead, radio clipped to his chest and his journey diagram folded in the cab beside him.

**Goal.** Operate his assigned journey safely and to schedule — follow the route and stopping pattern, depart and arrive on time, respond to instructions from control — and see, in one place, his assigned journeys, the route and stops, operational notices and the current disruption picture. When something happens on the line he wants one simple, structured way to raise it from the cab, without it pulling his attention off the train.

**Pain.** The reporting loop. When an incident, obstruction or fault happens he has no quick, structured way to raise it while stopped, so it leaves the cab only as a verbal report by radio or phone and is then re-keyed or re-broadcast by hand by controllers and station staff — slow, unstructured and easy to lose. Those reports never reach a shared, machine-readable source of truth, so control and passenger-facing channels cannot rely on them and the information lands late, inconsistently or not at all. He also gets little in return: no consolidated digital view of his assigned journeys, route, disruption picture or notices from control. Any tool has to stay minimal and must not demand his attention while the train is moving, and must survive weak connectivity without losing a report.

---

*Illustrative name and face. The goal and pain are taken from the driver-operator discovery.*
